Wage Transparency Laws by State

A growing number of states require employers to disclose a salary or wage range in every job posting — including PERM Sunday newspaper ads, since these are public job postings under state law. Below is the current list of states with these requirements.

15 states + DC require a range in the posting itself

3 more require disclosure on request or before an offer, not in the ad text.

States requiring a range in the posting
  • California
    15+ employees · effective Jan 1, 2023    California DIR →
  • Colorado
    All employers with 1+ CO employee · effective Jan 1, 2021    Colorado CDLE →
  • Hawaii
    50+ employees · effective Jan 1, 2024    Hawaii Employer Council →
  • Illinois
    15+ employees · effective Jan 1, 2025    Illinois Dept. of Labor →
  • Maine
    Check current threshold before posting · effective Jul 29, 2026    Maine Dept. of Labor →
  • Maryland
    All employers · effective Oct 1, 2024    Maryland Dept. of Labor →
  • Massachusetts
    25+ employees · effective Oct 29, 2025    Massachusetts.gov →
  • Minnesota
    30+ employees · effective Jan 1, 2025    Minnesota DLI →
  • New Jersey
    10+ employees · effective Jun 1, 2025    New Jersey DOL →
  • New York
    4+ employees · effective Sep 17, 2023    New York DOL →
  • Vermont
    5+ employees · effective Jul 1, 2025    Vermont Dept. of Labor →
  • Virginia
    All employers · effective Jul 1, 2026    Virginia LIS (HB636) →
  • Washington
    15+ employees · effective Jan 1, 2023    Washington RCW →
  • Washington, D.C.
    All employers · effective Jun 30, 2024    DC Dept. of Employment Services →
  • Delaware
    25+ employees · effective Sep 26, 2027 (not yet in effect)    Delaware Dept. of Labor →

This page is for planning purposes and is not legal advice. Thresholds, effective dates, and requirements change — confirm current requirements with your attorney before finalizing ad text. New states are added to this list as laws take effect.
